Product Plan · v3 Sept 2026 Working draft

Global Tailor

A mobile-first marketplace where tailors worldwide open a virtual shop and US customers build a bespoke garment — choose the cloth, the cut, the options — submit measurements, pick a shipping speed, and pay through the platform. The garment contract is customer ↔ tailor; Global Tailor facilitates the payment and takes a small fee.

Mobile-first · iPhone US-only launch · scale-ready Role-based auth Suits · Shirts · Pants Standardized measurements Type→material pricing Flat-rate shipping Stripe · Phase 2 Seed: 1 test tailor AI photo→tile
01

The core loop

What a customer does on their phone, from browsing to placing an order. Proving this loop end-to-end is the whole of Phase 1 — before a cent moves.

1

Browse

Shop by shop, by cut, or by material — filters for price, turnaround, rating. Server-rendered for discovery.

2

Build the garment · core feature

Garment type → material (a clean AI-normalized tile) → cut & options, with a live running total. Price ties through: a suit uses more cloth than a shirt.

3

Measure

A standardized, illustrated wizard per garment type. Saved as a reusable measurement profile. Tailor-custom asks and AR plug into the same schema later.

4

Choose shipping

Pick from the tailor's own flat-rate options (they ship it, they quote it — e.g. DHL). Adds to the total, sets the delivery estimate.

5

Place order

The order is assembled with every choice frozen onto it.

★
Phase 1 stops at a test order. Purely UI/flow validation — no payment and no tailor engagement (he does not cut cloth). Stripe checkout arrives in Phase 2 once the loop is proven on an iPhone.
02

Order lifecycle & the money hold

Once payments are live (Phase 2), a custom garment takes weeks and can't be returned — so funds are captured up front but released to the tailor in stages. That staged release is what funds trust and disputes.

05

From the tailor's photos to selectable tiles

1 · Raw upload

Every photo & measurement video the tailor gives us lands in one media table — URLs, never blobs.

2 · AI normalize

A vision model crops each fabric photo into a clean, consistent swatch tile and extracts color, pattern & material attributes.

3 · Human confirm

The tailor or an admin approves the tile and attributes before it goes live. AI proposes; a human signs off — never publish an unreviewed claim about a real product.

★
Start with a POC. Our test tailor shared only a subset, via photos, so a categorization pass is needed. Prove the pipeline on his materials, then tune the mix and accuracy bar with him.

07

The first two weeks

All about proving the app + auth + loop. Payments come later.

  1. Scaffold Next.js (mobile-first) + Supabase; wire auth + roles + RLS from the very start.

    Everything sits on the role model — build it first.

  2. Migrations for users/roles, shops, garment types, fabrics + media, flat-rate shipping.
  3. Tailor shop editor — profile, selectable types, fabrics, options, pricing, shipping — seeded with the test tailor's real data.
  4. Photo→tile POC on his material photos: AI normalize, then human confirm.
  5. Public shop + configurator ending in a test order — the whole loop, testable on an iPhone. Stripe gets wired in Phase 2.
